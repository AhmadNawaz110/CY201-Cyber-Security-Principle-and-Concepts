Module 1: Introduction to Risk Management

Core Concepts

  • Risk: The potential for loss or damage when a threat exploits a vulnerability.
  • Threat: A danger that could potentially harm a system (e.g., Hacker, Malware).
  • Vulnerability: A weakness in a system that can be exploited (e.g., Unpatched software).
  • Impact: The magnitude of harm if the risk materializes.
Think like an analyst: Risk is never zero. It's about business alignment. We manage risk to an acceptable level, not necessarily eliminate it entirely.

Case Study: Real-World Impact

Equifax Breach (2017)

The Incident: Personal data of 147 million people was exposed.

The Vulnerability: Unpatched Apache Struts framework (CVE-2017-5638).

The Failure: Lack of asset visibility and patch management, despite the patch being available 2 months prior.

Module 2: Risk Assessment & Analysis

Qualitative vs. Quantitative

  • Qualitative: Subjective (High/Medium/Low). Fast, good for high-level overview. Uses a Risk Matrix.
  • Quantitative: Objective ($ value, ALE - Annualized Loss Expectancy). Uses formulas: SLE * ARO = ALE.
Analyst Insight: In most corporate environments, we start with Qualitative to prioritize resources, then move to Quantitative for the top 5 critical risks to justify budget.

Module 3: Risk Mitigation Strategies

The Four Responses

Avoidance

Discontinue the activity causing risk.

Transfer

Insurance, outsourcing, SLAs.

Mitigate

Apply controls (Firewalls, Training).

Acceptance

Cost of mitigation > Cost of loss.

Frameworks & Controls

Security controls are mapped to standards like NIST SP 800-53 or ISO 27001.

  • Administrative: Policies, training, hiring.
  • Technical: Encryption, MFA, IDS/IPS.
  • Physical: Badges, guards, fences.

Module 6: GRC Integration

Governance, Risk, Compliance

GRC ensures security aligns with business goals and legal requirements.

  • Policies: High-level statements of intent.
  • Procedures: Step-by-step instructions.
  • Standards: ISO 27001, NIST CSF, PCI-DSS.
  • Regulations: GDPR, HIPAA, CCPA.

Key Takeaways:

  • Risk Management is a continuous lifecycle, not a one-time project.
  • Quantitative analysis justifies budget; Qualitative analysis guides priority.
  • Incident Response relies heavily on Preparation and Lessons Learned.
  • GRC bridges the gap between technical security and business objectives.
